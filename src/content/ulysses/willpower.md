We talk about willpower like it's character. You either have it or you don't, and if you don't, that says something about you.

I think it works more like a bank account. You start the day with some. Every small no costs a bit. No to the second coffee, no to the angry reply, no to checking your phone in the meeting. By the evening there isn't much left.

I should be honest here: the science is messier than that. The famous studies about willpower running out like a tired muscle haven't held up well when other labs tried to repeat them. But you don't need a lab to notice that you at 11pm make different choices than you at 9am.

And you're not the only one who's noticed.

The supermarket puts the sweets by the checkout, where you're tired and stuck in a queue. The streaming service starts the next episode before you've decided whether you want it. Nobody is testing your virtue at 11pm. They're checking your balance.

> You don't need more willpower. You need fewer moments where you have to use it.

The people who look disciplined are usually just good at avoiding the fight. The snacks never make it into the house. The phone charges in another room. They're not stronger than you. They set things up so they didn't have to be.

Which, if you think about it, is what the supermarket did too.
