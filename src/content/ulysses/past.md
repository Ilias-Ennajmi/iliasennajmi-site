Every time you recall a memory, you do not play it back — you rebuild it, and you rebuild it to fit the person doing the remembering today. The past is not a fixed place you visit. It is a story under constant, quiet revision, edited to keep the present bearable.

This is not lying. It is maintenance. A decision that looked reckless at the time becomes, in memory, brave or inevitable, because the alternative — carrying it as a plain mistake — costs too much to hold. We give ourselves the version of the past we can afford to live beside.

> We do not remember what happened. We remember what we needed it to mean.

Knowing this will not make your memory accurate. But it can make you gentler with other people’s versions, and a little more suspicious of your own — especially the ones that flatter you. The memories that make you the hero deserve the most scrutiny.
