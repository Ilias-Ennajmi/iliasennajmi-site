Nobody wants a drill, the old line goes, they want a hole. It is truer to say nobody wants the hole either — they want the shelf, and the kind of person who has the sort of home where the shelf is up. The product is the last, least interesting link in a chain that starts with who someone is trying to become.

This is why feature lists persuade so poorly. A spec sheet answers a question the buyer is not really asking. They are not weighing your gigabytes; they are checking whether owning this makes them more like the person they picture when they picture themselves at their best.

> Sell the after-photo of the person, not the before-photo of the product.

The best positioning barely describes the thing at all. It describes a self, and quietly lets the product become the cost of admission. Get the identity right and the features become evidence. Get it wrong and no feature will save you.
